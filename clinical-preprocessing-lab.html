Problem 01 • The Clinical Informatics Reality
Missing Not At Random (MNAR)

Informed Missingness: The Lab That Was Never Ordered

In traditional data science, missing values are assumed to be random flaws to be patched. In medical informatics, the act of ordering a test is itself an informative diagnostic feature. Sicker patients receive frequent arterial punctures and electrolyte panels; healthy patients have vast swathes of empty fields.

Type 1

MCAR (Completely at Random)

Rare in medicine.

A blood tube accidentally drops and shatters on the floor, or a courier van gets a flat tire. The probability of missingness is entirely independent of the patient's condition.

Safe for: Complete Case Analysis
Type 2

MAR (Missing at Random)

Conditionally explainable.

Elderly patients (> 80 yrs) receive echocardiograms far more frequently than young adults. Once we condition on the recorded feature Age, the missingness is explained.

Safe for: MICE & KNN Imputation
Type 3

MNAR (Not at Random)

The dominant EHR pattern.

A patient in diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) has blood glucose measured every 30 minutes. A post-op orthopedic knee patient has it checked once. The missing values depend directly on the unmeasured physiological severity!

Required: Missing Indicator Columns

Problem 03 • Power-Law Clinical Chemistry
Log & Box-Cox Transforms

Handling Extreme Positive Skew in Inflammatory Markers

Laboratory biomarkers such as C-Reactive Protein (CRP), D-Dimer, and Serum Ferritin follow a severe log-normal distribution. 90% of patients have baseline values under $5\text{ mg/L}$, but severe sepsis or COVID-19 cytokine storms drive values into the hundreds ($300+\text{ mg/L}$).

Serum CRP Distribution Transformation

Why Linear Models Collapse on Raw Labs

When fitting Linear or Logistic Regression, high-leverage extreme outliers dominate the loss gradient \(\nabla L\). The model overfits to the few extreme cases while losing predictive fidelity across the 95% baseline population.

\(y_{\text{transformed}} = \ln(x + 1) \quad \text{or} \quad y^{(\lambda)} = \frac{x^\lambda - 1}{\lambda}\)

Problem 04 • Dynamic Feature Engineering
Critical Care Feature Extraction

Temporal Feature Synthesis: Deriving the Shock Index

Static vital sign snapshots fail to capture acute occult hypovolemia. By engineering the Shock Index (SI)—the physiological ratio of Heart Rate to Systolic Blood Pressure—clinicians and ML models can identify circulatory shock hours before arterial hypotension develops.

24-Hour Patient Hemodynamic Deterioration & Shock Index

Notice that while BP remains nominally acceptable (> 95 mmHg), the Shock Index spikes above critical threshold 0.9 at Hour 10!

Shock Index > 0.9 = Occult Shock
Normal SI (0.5 – 0.7): Stable hemodynamics. Normal left ventricular stroke volume.
Elevated SI (0.7 – 0.9): Compensated tachycardia; early fluid resuscitation indicated.
Severe SI (> 0.9): Impending decompensation, massive transfusion protocol or ICU admission required.
Problem 05 • High-Cardinality Categoricals
70,000+ Billing Codes

The Dimensionality Trap: Preprocessing ICD-10 Diagnostic Codes

Applying One-Hot Encoding to ICD-10 diagnosis codes generates over 70,000 sparse binary columns, causing catastrophic overfitting and out-of-memory errors. Here are the three industry-standard clinical compression strategies:

1

Hierarchical Tree Roll-Up

Truncate sub-codes (e.g. I21.09 ST-elevation myocardial infarction) to 3-digit categories (I21 Acute MI) or 22 major WHO chapters (I00-I99 Circulatory diseases).

Reduces 70k → 22 features
2

Charlson Comorbidity Index

Map ICD codes into 17 weighted clinical condition scores (e.g., Congestive Heart Failure = 1, Metastatic Solid Tumor = 6) based on 10-year mortality risk.

Clinically validated summary score
3

Bayesian Target Encoding

Replace each diagnosis with the empirical probability of the target outcome (e.g., in-hospital mortality), smoothed with an empirical Bayesian prior for rare diseases.

Zero sparse matrix overhead
