Section 01 • Data Partitioning

1. The Core Concept: Train / Test Split

The golden rule of medical artificial intelligence: never test a clinical model on the patients it learned from. If you do, the algorithm simply memorizes hospital chart numbers (overfitting). It appears 99% accurate on historical charts, but fails dangerously on incoming emergency patients.

📚 Training Cohort (80% of Past Encounters)

The study cohort. The algorithm reviews patient vitals (X_train) alongside confirmed gold-standard pathology or discharge outcomes (y_train) to calibrate its internal decision weights.

🧪 Holdout Test Cohort (20% Vault Partition)

The clinical board exam. Locked in an air-gapped vault during development. The model never glimpses this cohort until final evaluation, ensuring completely objective performance validation.

Stratified Hospital Splitting in Python

Using stratify=y guarantees that rare clinical conditions (e.g., 3% in-hospital cardiac arrest prevalence) are balanced identically across both train and test splits, preventing sampling distortion. 

from sklearn.model_selection import train_test_split

# stratify=y preserves exact disease prevalence ratio
X_train, X_test, y_train, y_test = train_test_split(
    X, y,
    test_size=0.20,
    random_state=42,
    stratify=y
)

Section 04 • Continuous Targets

4. Regression Metrics (Continuous Targets)

When models predict continuous medical measurements (post-operative length of stay in days, arterial blood pressure in mmHg, insulin units), we evaluate prediction errors using MAE, RMSE, and $R^2$.

Points tightly clustered along the 45° dashed diagonal indicate superior clinical calibration.

Which Metric Should Clinicians Report?

  • Mean Absolute Error (MAE): Average error in original clinical units (e.g. ±3.4 mmHg blood pressure). Highly intuitive for doctors and robust against outliers.
  • Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE): Penalizes massive clinical errors heavily due to squaring. Critical when missing by 20 days is 100x worse than missing by 2 days.
  • R-Squared ($R^2$ Variance Explained): Percentage of total patient physiological variability explained by the model (e.g. $R^2 = 0.85$ means 85% of variance captured).
Section 05 • Diagnostic Metrics

5. Classification Metrics (Sensitivity, Specificity & The Accuracy Trap)

In retail data science, accuracy is king. In healthcare, raw accuracy is a dangerous trap. A screening algorithm with 98% accuracy can be utterly worthless if it achieves that accuracy by marking 100% of patients as "Healthy"—resulting in a 100% death rate for the 2% of patients with acute myocardial infarction.

The Clinical Confusion Matrix Anatomy

True Positive (TP) Sick patient correctly flagged. Life-saving early treatment initiated!
False Positive (FP) Healthy patient alarmed. Causes unnecessary biopsy / alarm fatigue.
False Negative (FN) Sick patient sent home. Catastrophic delayed diagnosis or death!
True Negative (TN) Healthy patient cleared. Avoids hospital bed congestion.

Precision, Recall & F1-Score

Balancing sensitivity (catching every sick patient) vs. precision (minimizing alarm fatigue).

Section 06 • Robust Generalization

6. Cross-Validation & Patient-Level Leakage Prevention

A single random train/test split can be lucky or unlucky. K-Fold Cross-Validation cycles through 5 or 10 distinct partitions to establish true statistical confidence intervals. However, healthcare informatics requires a crucial architectural safeguard: GroupKFold.

🚨

The Number One Medical AI Blunder: Patient Contamination

Suppose Patient #42 stays in the ICU for 3 days and has 12 Chest X-rays taken. If you use standard random K-Fold, 10 scans of Patient #42 end up in the training set and 2 scans end up in the test set.

The neural network achieves 99.4% accuracy—not because it diagnosed pneumonia, but because it recognized Patient #42's pacemaker, spinal curvature, and unique rib cage! When deployed on new patients in another ward, accuracy crashes completely!

Rule: All records of the same patient MUST remain strictly on one side of the split using GroupKFold!

Cross-Validation Stability per Fold

Low variance across folds demonstrates algorithm stability across different clinical sub-cohorts.

Patient-Grouped Cross-Validation Implementation

from sklearn.model_selection import GroupKFold, cross_val_score
from sklearn.ensemble import RandomForestClassifier

# patient_id array ensures all scans of a patient stay in ONE fold
gkf = GroupKFold(n_splits=5)
model = RandomForestClassifier(random_state=42)

scores = cross_val_score(model, X, y, groups=patient_ids, cv=gkf, scoring='roc_auc')
print(f"Leak-Free Mean ROC-AUC: {scores.mean():.3f} ± {scores.std():.3f}")
