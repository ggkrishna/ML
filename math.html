Section 01 • Formalization

1. A Formal Introduction to Learning & Empirical Risk

In computer science, machine learning is formally defined as searching an infinite hypothesis space \(\mathcal{H}\) to find an optimal mapping function \(f: \mathcal{X} \to \mathcal{Y}\) that maps patient clinical signs \(\mathbf{x}\) to clinical target outcomes \(y\).

Supervised Learning (Task-Driven)

Risk Minimization

Given an EHR training cohort of \(N\) verified patient records \(\mathcal{D} = \{(\mathbf{x}_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^{N}\), we seek parameters \(\mathbf{w}^*\) that minimize the Empirical Risk:

$$R_{\text{emp}}(f) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \mathcal{L}(f(\mathbf{x}_i), y_i)$$

Clinical Translation: Add up the clinical penalties across all \(N\) hospital patients and divide by \(N\) to obtain average hospital error.

The Generalization Hazard: We minimize empirical risk on past hospital patients (\(\mathcal{D}_{\text{train}}\)), but our true clinical mission is minimizing True Risk \(\mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{x},y)\sim \mathcal{P}}[\mathcal{L}(f(\mathbf{x}), y)]\) on future unseen patients!

Loss Function \(\mathcal{L}\): Quantifies prediction discrepancy (e.g., Mean Squared Error for blood pressure estimation, Binary Cross-Entropy for 30-day readmission).

Unsupervised Learning (Data-Driven)

Density Estimation

Given an unlabeled clinical dataset \(\mathcal{D} = \{\mathbf{x}_i\}_{i=1}^{N}\) without gold-standard diagnosis tags, the objective is to model the underlying probability distribution \(P(\mathbf{x})\) or discover geometric manifold topology:

  • • Clustering (Inertia Minimization): Partitioning ICU sepsis encounters into distinct physiological clusters to discover uncharacterized endotypes.
  • • Dimensionality Reduction: Compressing 20,000 gene expression dimensions \(\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{20000}\) into a 2D latent embedding \(\mathbf{z} \in \mathbb{R}^2\) using PCA or UMAP.
Clinical Purpose: Precision medicine phenotyping — grouping patients by molecular pathophysiology rather than broad symptom labels.

The Clinical Bias-Variance Decomposition

For any continuous clinical prediction task under squared error loss, expected prediction error breaks down mathematically into three irreducible clinical components:

$$\mathbb{E}[(y - \hat{f}(\mathbf{x}))^2] = \underbrace{\text{Bias}[\hat{f}(\mathbf{x})]^2}_{\text{Clinical Oversimplification}} + \underbrace{\text{Var}[\hat{f}(\mathbf{x})]}_{\text{Over-Sensitivity to Hospital Noise}} + \underbrace{\sigma_{\epsilon}^2}_{\text{Irreducible Biological Noise}}$$
1. High Bias (Underfitting)

The model is too primitive (e.g., a rigid rule stating "Sepsis if Temp > 38°C"). It fails to capture multi-organ pathophysiology and constantly misses septic shock in hypothermic patients.

2. High Variance (Overfitting)

The model has millions of parameters and memorizes Hospital Ward A's exact shift schedules and thermometer calibration errors. It gets 99.8% training accuracy but fails completely when tested at Hospital Ward B.

3. Irreducible Noise (\(\sigma_{\epsilon}^2\))

Unmeasured biological variation, unknown genetic polymorphisms, or EHR entry typing mistakes. No mathematical model, no matter how complex, can eliminate this baseline uncertainty.

Section 02 • Parametric Models

2. Core Parametric Models: Regression & Optimization

Parametric models assume a specific functional form \(f(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{w})\) governed by learnable parameter weights \(\mathbf{w}\). Here we examine how linear models, logit transforms, and iterative optimization operate under the hood.

Linear Regression

Ordinary Least Squares

Linear combination of clinical vitals with baseline intercept \(w_0\):

$$\hat{y} = w_0 + \sum_{j=1}^{d} w_j x_j = \mathbf{w}^T\mathbf{x}$$

Mean Squared Error (MSE) objective function:

$$L(\mathbf{w}) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} (y_i - \mathbf{w}^T\mathbf{x}_i)^2$$

Closed-Form Analytical Solution: Setting \(\nabla_{\mathbf{w}} L(\mathbf{w}) = 0\) yields \(\mathbf{w}^* = (\mathbf{X}^T\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}^Ty\).

Clinical Bottleneck: Inverting \((\mathbf{X}^T\mathbf{X})\) requires \(\mathcal{O}(d^3)\) operations. For 50,000 genomic features or collinear EHR labs, the matrix cannot be inverted! This forces us to use Gradient Descent.

Logistic Regression

Cross-Entropy Loss

Sigmoid activation mapping linear risk score \(z = \mathbf{w}^T\mathbf{x}\) into a valid \([0, 1]\) probability:

$$P(y=1 \mid \mathbf{x}) = \sigma(\mathbf{w}^T\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-\mathbf{w}^T\mathbf{x}}}$$

Binary Cross-Entropy (Negative Log-Likelihood):

$$L(\mathbf{w}) = -\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \left[ y_i \ln(\hat{y}_i) + (1-y_i)\ln(1-\hat{y}_i) \right]$$

Why Not Mean Squared Error? If a patient has septic shock (\(y=1\)) and our model predicts a calm \(\hat{y}=0.01\), \(-\ln(0.01) \approx 4.60\) (massive penalty). Cross-entropy penalizes confident medical blunders exponentially!

No closed-form solution exists for logistic regression — weights must be solved iteratively via Gradient Descent.

Section 03 • Non-Parametric

3. Non-Parametric: K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) & Distance Geometry

Unlike parametric models, KNN does not learn an explicit weight vector during training. It memorizes the entire clinical cohort and defers calculation to bedside query time, comparing a new patient against past historical cases in multidimensional space.

Mathematical Formalization of KNN

Metric space distance calculation and posterior probability estimation.

Metric Space

Distance Metrics in \(\mathbb{R}^n\) between Patient \(\mathbf{p}\) and Patient \(\mathbf{q}\):

$$d_2(\mathbf{p}, \mathbf{q}) = \sqrt{ \sum_{j=1}^{n} (p_j - q_j)^2 } \quad \text{(Euclidean } L_2\text{)}$$
$$d_1(\mathbf{p}, \mathbf{q}) = \sum_{j=1}^{n} |p_j - q_j| \quad \text{(Manhattan } L_1\text{)}$$

Class Posterior Probability within neighborhood \(\mathcal{N}_K(\mathbf{x})\):

$$P(y = c \mid \mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{N}_K(\mathbf{x})} \mathbb{I}(y_i = c)$$
⚠️ The Curse of Dimensionality in Health Informatics

In pure mathematics, as dimension \(d \to \infty\), the volume of a hypercube concentrates entirely in its corners, and the distance between the nearest neighbor and the farthest neighbor approaches zero:

$$\lim_{d \to \infty} \frac{\text{dist}_{\max} - \text{dist}_{\min}}{\text{dist}_{\min}} = 0$$

Clinical Consequence: If an informatics student feeds 20,000 raw RNA sequencing genes or 500 unselected EHR ICD codes into KNN without dimensionality reduction (PCA or UMAP), every single patient in the hospital appears almost exactly equidistant from every other patient! The distance metric collapses, and the algorithm degrades into coin-flip chance.

Section 04 • Information Theory

4. Interpretable Decisions: Trees, Entropy & Random Forests

Decision trees partition the feature space into hyper-rectangles using information-theoretic splitting criteria. They mimic clinical triage algorithms, asking sequential questions to maximize diagnostic purity.

Splitting Metrics: Gini & Entropy

Information Gain

Gini Impurity (Misclassification Probability):

$$G(p) = 1 - \sum_{k=1}^{C} p_k^2$$

Shannon Entropy (Bits of Clinical Uncertainty):

$$H(p) = -\sum_{k=1}^{C} p_k \log_2(p_k)$$

Information Gain maximized at each clinical split:

$$\Delta H = H(\text{Parent}) - \left[ \frac{N_L}{N} H(D_L) + \frac{N_R}{N} H(D_R) \right]$$
Pure Clinical Leaf Nodes: A triage leaf node where 100% of patients have Acute MI has \(p_1 = 1.0, p_2 = 0\), yielding \(Gini = 1 - (1^2 + 0^2) = 0.0\) and \(Entropy = 0.0\) bits (zero medical doubt).

Random Forest: Bagging & Variance Reduction

Decorrelation

A Random Forest trains \(B\) individual unpruned trees and averages their votes to reduce predictive variance:

$$\text{Var}(\bar{f}_{\text{rf}}) = \rho \sigma^2 + \frac{1 - \rho}{B} \sigma^2$$

Clinical Decorrelation (\(\rho\)): If every tree evaluated all features, every tree would split on Troponin first, producing correlated trees (\(\rho \to 1\)).

By forcing each split to randomly consider only \(m = \lfloor \sqrt{d} \rfloor\) candidate biomarkers, trees look at ECG, Blood Pressure, Age, and History separately, driving \(\rho \downarrow 0\)!

Majority Aggregation: \(\hat{y}_{\text{rf}} = \text{mode}\left( \{ f_b(\mathbf{x}) \}_{b=1}^{B} \right)\)

Section 05 • Deep Architectures

5. Neural Networks & Multivariate Matrix Calculus

Neural networks are composite non-linear function approximators optimized by backpropagating the gradient of loss via the multivariate calculus chain rule.

The Forward & Backward Pass

Matrix Calculus

Layer activation forward computation with non-linear activation \(g\):

$$\mathbf{a}^{(l)} = g\left( \mathbf{W}^{(l)}\mathbf{a}^{(l-1)} + \mathbf{b}^{(l)} \right)$$

Backpropagation gradient via chain rule for weight matrix \(\mathbf{W}^{(l)}\):

$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \mathbf{W}^{(l)}} = \mathbf{\delta}^{(l)} (\mathbf{a}^{(l-1)})^T, \quad \mathbf{\delta}^{(l)} = \left( (\mathbf{W}^{(l+1)})^T \mathbf{\delta}^{(l+1)} \right) \odot g'(\mathbf{z}^{(l)})$$

Clinical Translation: Backpropagation is automated clinical audit. If an algorithm misdiagnoses a stroke patient, the chain rule calculates exactly how much blame belongs to each intermediate layer of synthetic biomarkers.

Modern Activations: ReLU \(\max(0, z)\) prevents vanishing gradients; Softmax \(\frac{e^{z_k}}{\sum e^{z_j}}\) ensures multi-class differential diagnosis outputs sum to exactly 100%.

Specialized Clinical Architectures

Problem Fit

Suitability across healthcare modalities:

Clinical Fit: CNNs dominate radiology (2D/3D convolutions for CT/X-Ray); Transformers dominate unstructured EHR clinical notes via Self-Attention \(\text{softmax}\left(\frac{\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)\mathbf{V}\).
Section 06 • Advanced Models

6. Beyond the Basics: Clustering, Support Vector Machines & Bayes' Rule

Analytical formulations for unsupervised K-Means inertia minimization, Support Vector Machine quadratic programming, and Bayes' Theorem in diagnostic decision-making.

K-Means Clustering

Expectation-Maximization

Minimizing within-cluster sum of squares (WCSS / Inertia):

$$J = \sum_{k=1}^{K} \sum_{\mathbf{x}_i \in C_k} ||\mathbf{x}_i - \mathbf{\mu}_k||^2$$

Alternates: 1. Assignment step \(\arg\min_k ||\mathbf{x}_i - \mathbf{\mu}_k||\) & 2. Centroid update \(\mathbf{\mu}_k = \frac{1}{|C_k|}\sum \mathbf{x}_i\).

Support Vector Machines

Margin Maximization

Primal constrained margin maximization:

$$\min_{\mathbf{w}, b} \frac{1}{2} ||\mathbf{w}||^2 \quad \text{s.t.} \quad y_i(\mathbf{w}^T\mathbf{x}_i + b) \ge 1, \quad \forall i$$

Dual formulation solved via Lagrange multipliers: \(\max_{\alpha} \sum \alpha_i - \frac{1}{2}\sum \alpha_i \alpha_j y_i y_j K(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_j)\).

Bayes' Theorem & The Clinical Screening Paradox

Why a 95% accurate AI model can produce 84% false alarms on rare hospital conditions.

Probabilistic Inference

Bayes' Theorem is the cornerstone of clinical evidence-based diagnosis. When a diagnostic AI generates a positive alert (\(+\)), what is the real probability that the patient actually has the disease (\(D\))?

$$P(D \mid +) = \frac{P(+ \mid D) \cdot P(D)}{P(+ \mid D) \cdot P(D) + P(+ \mid \neg D) \cdot P(\neg D)}$$

Where \(P(+ \mid D)\) is Clinical Sensitivity, \(P(+ \mid \neg D) = 1 - \text{Specificity}\) is the False Positive Rate, and \(P(D)\) is the Prior Disease Prevalence in your hospital population.

🚨 The Base Rate Fallacy & EHR Alert Fatigue

If an AI screening algorithm with 95% Sensitivity and 95% Specificity is deployed to detect a rare condition (prevalence 1%), many clinicians assume a positive test means a 95% chance of disease.

The mathematical truth: The actual probability is only ~16.1%! Over 83% of positive alerts are false alarms, leading straight to dangerous clinician alert fatigue.
